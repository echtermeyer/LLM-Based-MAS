\begin{abstract}

Multi-agent debate is promoted as a way to make language models reason better
together, but it is evaluated almost entirely by final accuracy, which reveals
nothing about how a group arrives at its answer or whether it is deliberating
soundly. We study cooperative LLM debate as a dynamical system and define
\suitename{} (Self-Organization Motif Analysis), a suite of detectors for four
self-organization motifs, self-reinforcement, limit cycles,
bistability, and dominance, each measured from interaction logs against a
principled null and applied to 21{,}000 debates across two benchmarks. Their
prevalence varies sharply: self-reinforcement is universal, bistability common,
dominance behaviourally present, and genuine limit cycles rare and confined to one
topology, an informative pattern in itself.

Our central result is that the four motifs are faces of one mechanism: the debate
predominantly amplifies the vote distribution it starts with rather than reasoning
a new answer into existence. Self-reinforcement is universal and drives
convergence, yet is blind to correctness, strengthening whichever option leads at
the outset. A causal seeding experiment on GPQA confirms the amplifier reading:
inserting a single correct agent into an otherwise-wrong opening raises accuracy
roughly thirteen-fold, while the system almost never recovers a correct answer that
no agent initially held. The structural design choices we varied, communication topology and
memory window, change convergence speed, worst-case runtime, and influence
concentration, but not accuracy, which is largely fixed before deliberation begins.
The internal signals a user might trust are misleading: confidence rises regardless
of correctness, and early unanimity on biased tasks predicts wrong answers.

Beyond detecting the motifs, we show that each detector, computed from ordinary
logs, can serve as a diagnostic of deliberative health, and we distil the findings
into design principles graded by evidential strength, as groundwork for a diagnostic
suite. All claims are bounded to the tested setting, a homogeneous, cooperative,
3--4 agent debate on one model family, and the absence of a motif is treated as an
informative result throughout.

\end{abstract}
