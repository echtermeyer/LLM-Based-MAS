This thesis asked whether the dynamical vocabulary of complex systems can be
applied empirically to LLM-based multi-agent debate, and what such a treatment
reveals that outcome metrics cannot. We built a controlled cooperative debate
system, developed four literature-grounded motif detectors, and ran the full grid
on two deliberately contrasting benchmarks, comprising 30{,}000 debates. We
answer the three research questions in turn.

\paragraph{RQ1: measurement.}
The motifs are detectable, at very different prevalences, and each detector
distinguishes genuine structure from a principled null. Self-reinforcement is
\emph{universal}: positive confidence trends dominate in every configuration
($p_{\mathrm{SR}} \in [0.73, 0.88]$, all cluster-bootstrap intervals excluding the
$0.5$ baseline). Bistability is \emph{common and task-intrinsic}: most task--config
cells show genuine multi-attractor coexistence ($N_{\mathrm{eff}} \geq 1.5$ in
$76.3\,\%$), and this is a stable property of the task rather than the
configuration. Dominance appears as \emph{behavioural influence concentration}
above the topology-keyed null even in fully-connected debate. Limit cycles are
\emph{rare but real}: $99\,\%$ of sequences converge, and the small residue that
does not yields $47$ agent-level and $32$ system-level statistically flagged cases,
of which $33$ and $7$ respectively are genuine period-2 oscillations on manual
inspection, arising almost exclusively under the star
topology through a hub-driven mechanism. The absence of genuine cycles in
fully-connected debate is itself a clean result rather than a measurement failure.

\paragraph{RQ2: characterisation.}
The two structural axes shape the \emph{dynamics} but not the \emph{correctness}
of debate. Topology is the dominant structural lever: the star is slower,
concentrates influence roughly threefold, and is the sole source of genuine limit
cycles, while the fully-connected topology produces stronger self-reinforcement and
faster convergence. The memory window has only secondary effects on convergence
speed and none on accuracy, and bistability is invariant to both axes.
What governs outcomes is the \emph{composition of the opening votes}: motifs are
informative as diagnostics of a dynamical process, not as guarantees of quality.
Self-reinforcement is the convergence mechanism everywhere (task-level
$\rho(\bar\beta,\text{rounds}) \in [-0.89, -0.73]$), yet it amplifies the initial
vote distribution rather than selecting for the correct answer. Dominance is
helpful when every agent sees every other but harmful when influence is forced
through a single hub. Bistability marks the tasks where convergence is least
reliable, and limit cycles mark pure efficiency failures. The motifs thus form an
explanatory layer beneath accuracy, and are the basis for the tiered,
empirically-grounded design principles of \autoref{sec:discussion:design}.

\paragraph{RQ3: causation.}
The outcome is largely determined before deliberation begins. Observationally,
whether the ground-truth option is present in the opening votes is the strongest
predictor of accuracy; causally, the seeded-initialisation experiment shows that
placing one correct agent into an otherwise-wrong opening raises accuracy roughly
thirteen-fold and in every task tested, while the system almost never manufactures
a correct answer no agent initially held. The overarching finding, stated with its
scope, is that \emph{within a homogeneous, cooperative, 4-agent debate on one
model family across two benchmarks}, deliberation functions predominantly as a
selective amplifier of its initial vote distribution rather than as a mechanism
that reasons a new answer into existence.


Multi-agent debate is often presented as a way to make language models reason
better together \citep{du2024improving, liang2024encouraging}.
Our results suggest a more precise and more cautious picture: in the homogeneous
cooperative setting we studied, the debate mostly amplifies what its members
already believe.
The \suitename{} suite operationalises this insight into four measurable detectors,
each computable from ordinary interaction logs; its broader value is as groundwork
for a diagnostic instrument that would let a practitioner check, before trusting
the group's output, which dynamical regime the deliberation is actually in.
