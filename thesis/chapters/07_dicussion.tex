\autoref{sec:results} reported what the system does. This section asks what it
means, in four steps. Section~\ref{sec:results:synthesis} explains \emph{why} the
system behaves as it does by locating it in the vocabulary of
\autoref{sec:bg:complex}. Section~\ref{sec:discussion:interpretation} asks what
that behaviour \emph{costs}, by measuring the debate against the vote it started
from. Section~\ref{sec:discussion:design} asks what a practitioner can
\emph{do} about it. Section~\ref{sec:discussion:limitations} states the bounds on
all three together with the work that would remove them.


\subsection{The Missing Ingredient}
\label{sec:results:synthesis}

This subsection answers why the system behaves as it does, and it does so by
returning to the definition of self-organization rather than by adding new
measurements. Previously, in \autoref{sec:bg:complex} introduced the four ingredients that Camazine and
colleagues identify as recurring in self-organizing systems, which are positive
feedback, negative feedback, fluctuations, and repeated local interaction. Three
of the four are clearly present in our results. Positive feedback is self-reinforcement,
which appears in every one of the twelve combinations of dataset, topology, and
memory window at a prevalence between $0.733$ and $0.881$ against a null of
$0.5$, and which is the engine that makes the debate settle, since tasks with
steeper confidence slopes reach consensus in fewer rounds in every combination we
tested. Fluctuations are visible as multistability, since $76\,\%$ of the $600$
task and configuration cells admit more than one stable outcome across
repetitions of the same question. Repeated local interaction is the debate
protocol itself. Thus, the second ingredient, negative feedback, is the one that is missing. 
Nothing in the protocol bounds an early lead. No agent is rewarded for holding out, the group
pays no cost for converging quickly, and confidence climbs fastest in exactly
those repetitions where the debate has least to resolve, which are the ones that
never change their votes and the ones that start unanimous.

That absence is the finding. A system with amplification and no damping does not
deliberate its way to an answer, it magnifies whatever asymmetry it starts with.
This also shows that \suitename{} divides the problem in a useful way, because the four detectors turn out to measure not four phenomena but one mechanism seen from four angles.
Self-reinforcement is the view within a stable segment, where an agent
grows more certain of a position it already held rather than of anything the
exchange revealed. Dominance is the view within a run, where influence
concentrates above the topology-keyed null in all but one of the hundred task and
benchmark combinations even though every agent in a fully connected run is wired
identically, so the group reliably elects a hierarchy that the structure never
specified. Multistability is the view across repetitions, where the same question
lands in different basins on different runs because the opening draw differs, and
it counts the basins that self-reinforcement then collapses into one. Limit
cycles are the boundary case where the magnification never terminates, and they
are informative precisely because they are almost absent, since nearly every
sequence reaches a fixed point and the $47$ agent level and $32$ system level
flagged cases sit almost entirely in the star topology. That the fully connected
topology produces no genuine cycles is a clean statement about cooperative
protocol design rather than a measurement failure, because without a single node
that aggregates all peer signals there is no way to reconstruct the opposing
majority after every flip.

Read this way the four motifs yield one claim rather than four findings. Within
the system we tested, deliberation works predominantly as a selective amplifier
of its initial vote distribution rather than as a mechanism that reasons a new
answer into existence. The claim is not novel in kind.
\citet{oh2026entrenchment} name homogenised dynamics that amplify the majority
view regardless of correctness as a root cause of belief entrenchment, and
\citet{estornell2024multillm} derive the same conclusion theoretically from
response homogeneity. What \suitename{} adds is measurement. Each face of the
mechanism is separately detectable from ordinary interaction logs and each was
tested against a null that destroys the pattern and leaves everything else
intact, so a practitioner can check which regime a system is in rather than
having to assume it.


\subsection{A Lossy Amplifier}
\label{sec:discussion:interpretation}

This subsection asks what the missing ingredient costs, by measuring the debate
against the only baseline that isolates its contribution, which is the vote the
same four agents already held before talking.
The seeded experiment answers that directly, because it fixes the number of
correct agents in the opening profile and leaves the debate itself untouched.
Accuracy climbs from $1.8\,\%$ with no correct seed to $26.2\,\%$, $63.7\,\%$,
and $86.3\,\%$ as one, two, and three agents begin on the correct option, and it
does so in almost every individual task. Set against what a plurality vote at
round $0$ would already deliver, the pattern is not a uniform gain. Where the
correct answer starts behind, the debate helps, since a lone correct agent faces
a plurality baseline of zero and still wins a quarter of the time, and an even
split of two against two improves on a baseline of $38\,\%$. Where the correct
answer starts ahead, the debate erodes it, since a correct supermajority of three
that plurality voting would preserve with certainty is talked out of the right
answer in roughly one repetition in seven.

Debate is therefore better described not as majority voting with reasoning
attached but as a transformation that widens the distribution around the opening
signal. It moves outcomes away from the initial plurality in both directions,
which is useful exactly when that plurality is unreliable and harmful when it is
already good. This one property accounts for the two operating regimes we observe
and, we think, for much of the disagreement in the literature about whether
debate works. On GPQA the agents usually begin with the correct option present,
the plurality of the opening votes already reaches $40.5\,\%$, there is little
room above that baseline, and debate adds under two percentage points. On
HiddenBench the shared model bias drives the opening plurality below chance,
because the errors are correlated in the way that biased information sampling
produces in human hidden profile groups
\citep{stasser1985pooling, li2026hiddenbench}, and the same widening recovers
$18.5$ percentage points. The two benchmarks are not an easy and a hard version
of one task, they are the two sides of the crossing point where the
transformation stops paying, observed inside a single experimental setup. Results
reported on benchmarks that sit on one side or the other will disagree about
debate for reasons that have nothing to do with the protocol, which is consistent
with the finding that debate often fails to beat simple baselines
\citep{liu2025stopovervaluing} and that majority voting alone accounts for much
of the apparent gain \citep{zhang2025debateorvote}.

The motif signatures track the same crossing point, which is the strongest
internal evidence that one mechanism is at work. Multistability helps on
HiddenBench, where more coexisting attractors go with higher accuracy at
$\rho = +0.44$, because a competing basin gives the correct minority a foothold
against a dominant attractor that is wrong, while on GPQA the relationship is
weakly negative and not significant, which is what we would expect where the
single dominant basin is already the right one. Dominance reverses in the same
way. When the debate elects its own hub in a fully connected run, that hub
started correct far more often than a random agent does, and effective star runs
reach the highest accuracy of the four structural types on both benchmarks. When
the hub is imposed by the wiring instead, the fixed centre is no more likely to
be correct than anyone else, so it spreads the shared bias rather than correcting
it, and higher concentration then goes with lower accuracy.

The adversarial probe shows what the transformation actually tracks, and it is
not correctness. Replacing one of the two incorrect agents in the two seed
condition with an agent that keeps arguing for a wrong option throughout
collapses accuracy from $63.7\,\%$ to $10.0\,\%$, in every task, and leaves the
group below what a single correct agent with no adversary achieves. Two correct
agents out of four are not enough to hold the line against one that never moves.
The amplifier responds to persistence, and since a cooperative agent that is
right and an adversarial agent that is committed produce the same observable
signal, the protocol has no basis on which to tell them apart. Whatever lift
truth seeding provides is equally available to anyone willing to supply a
confident wrong answer instead.


\subsection{What to Measure Instead}
\label{sec:discussion:design}

The motivation for this thesis was that outcome metrics tell a practitioner
whether a configuration worked but never why, and give no way to separate a
healthy deliberation from a pathological one \citep{miehling2025agentic}. The
negative half of our answer is that the signals an operator would naturally reach
for instead are uninformative or inverted. Confidence rises monotonically across
rounds whether the final answer is correct or not, so a confident group is not a
reliable one. Unanimity is worse than uninformative on HiddenBench, where
repetitions that stop early lock in the shared wrong answer almost universally
and reach only $2.5\,\%$, because early consensus forecloses the exchange that the
benchmark requires. Debate length carries no signal either, since longer debates
are no more accurate. The same holds for the two structural knobs the literature
most often tunes \citep{li2024sparsetopology, mehdizadeh2026topologymemory}. Neither
topology nor memory window has an accuracy effect that survives correction for
multiple comparisons, and both are clearly visible in the dynamics, since the
star needs more rounds on both benchmarks, is the sole source of genuine limit
cycles, and concentrates influence by construction. Network type having no main
effect on coordination in an eight topology factorial
\citep{mehdizadeh2026topologymemory} and communication density failing to lift
accuracy above a non communicative baseline \citep{bertalanic2026costofconsensus}
point the same way, and the dissociation explains why topology tuning results in
the literature are mixed.

The positive half is that the composition of the opening votes is readable,
cheap, and decisive, and that one design choice does reach it. The effective
influence topology that emerges inside a fully connected run predicts accuracy
even though the prescribed topology does not, because the debate elects a hub
that disproportionately started correct while a prescribed hub is fixed before
the debate can select anyone. Whether that election happens depends on how
diverse the opening votes are, since stars almost never form when all four agents
start on the same option and roughly one run in five becomes a star when all four
start on different ones. Diverse initialisation is therefore the one lever in our
grid that touches accuracy, and it does so because it acts on the input to the
amplifier rather than on the amplifier itself. Enforced perspective diversity has
already been proposed as a remedy for entrenchment \citep{oh2026entrenchment},
and what we add is the mechanism behind it.

\autoref{tab:design:instruments} states the resulting guidance together with the
result it rests on, the strength of that evidence, and the quantity that checks
it. We do not deliver a validated diagnostic tool, and the table should be read
as a set of hypotheses with their provenance attached rather than as a
specification. What it does show is that every recommendation here is checkable
from ordinary interaction logs using a \suitename{} detector that has already
been tested against a null model on $30{,}000$ repetitions, which is what we mean
by groundwork for a diagnostic suite rather than the suite itself. Taken together
the guidance is deflationary, and usefully so. A system whose answer is largely
fixed before deliberation begins, that cannot manufacture a correct answer absent
from its inputs, and whose own confidence does not track correctness is one whose
reliability has to be established upstream in the agents that produce the opening
positions and externally through verification that does not trust the group,
rather than through longer or more elaborate debate.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:discussion:limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion:future}

The first limitation is the scope of the experimental grid. We varied
communication topology, memory window, task difficulty, and benchmark, and in the
seeded experiment the composition of the opening votes, but we did not vary the
base model, the group size, the homogeneity of the agents, or the cooperative
framing. Every result reported here is therefore a statement about a homogeneous,
cooperative, four agent debate on one model family, and nothing in it establishes
what happens outside that setting. Each of these fixed choices is a direction for
further work. Group size is the most immediate, since $N = 4$ with three or four
answer options leaves a state space small enough that votes repeat and influence
concentrates by chance alone, which is why every detector required a null in the
first place, and a larger group would relax that constraint while letting the
effective topology analysis distinguish structures that four agents cannot
express. The structural knobs are narrow for a related reason, since fully
connected and star are the two extremes of a much larger space, and intermediate
structures such as rings, sparse or layered graphs, and dynamically rewiring ones
would show whether hub driven cycles are specific to a single aggregator or arise
wherever information is funnelled through a bottleneck. The memory window we
tested is short relative to the round cap, and at a sufficiently large $W$ a
spoke in a star can reconstruct the whole exchange from accumulated hub
summaries, at which point the structural difference between the topologies
largely disappears, so extending $W$ upward would locate the point at which the
distinction stops mattering. Agent heterogeneity is the axis the critical
literature considers decisive, since it identifies model diversity rather than
debate itself as the reliable source of gains
\citep{liu2025stopovervaluing, chen2024reconcile} and capability asymmetry as a
driver of harmful herding \citep{wynn2025talk}.

The second limitation is the reach of the causal evidence. The seeded experiment
is our only intervention and it was run on GPQA, so the amplifier claim is causal
there and observational on HiddenBench, where it rests on the association between
the presence of the ground truth in the opening votes and the final answer. The
diverse initialisation lever is observational throughout, because we measure that
the probability of an effective star rises with opening diversity across
naturally occurring runs rather than manipulating that diversity ourselves, and
the maximum diversity condition holds only six runs on HiddenBench. The effective
topology analysis is further confined to fully connected runs, since a leaf in a
star can only ever be converted by the centre, and its four types are separated
by thresholds on the share of influence captured by the top one or two agents, so
the rare types at $2.3\,\%$ and $5.4\,\%$ of runs carry real uncertainty.
Two interventions would address this. Seeding each agent with a distinct opening
answer would test the diversity lever directly, and perturbing or removing the
elected hub would establish whether its influence is causal or whether it proxies
for question specific expertise.

The third limitation is what \suitename{} is able to certify. All four detectors
identify signatures rather than dynamical objects. A positive confidence slope is
not a proof of a feedback loop, a recurrence test flags periodic structure rather
than a mathematically certified limit cycle, $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ counts coexisting
endpoints rather than a certified attractor landscape, and the dominance index
records where conversions concentrate rather than who caused them. Two of the
detectors also have a known direction of error. The self-reinforcement prevalence
may be inflated, because an agent whose confidence falls is more likely to flip
its vote and end the segment, which censors negative slopes, and a matched null
that models the flip hazard would show how much of the excess above the baseline
is genuine. The dominance graph is built from vote adoptions during the
\updatephase{} phase, which is precisely where the protocol instructs agents to
update, so a strong hub to spoke edge may reflect persuasion or merely the
routing of information. Removal based attribution, which estimates an agent's
contribution by masking its messages
\citep{agentsthatmatter2026, cui2025introspecloo}, would
replace the concentration index with an interventional measure of influence and
is the natural successor to what we report here.

The fourth limitation concerns what the reported rates describe. The tasks were
screened for outcome variation across repetitions, so the prevalences we report
characterise the regime in which the outcome is genuinely contested rather than
the full distribution of questions. This follows from the objective, which was to
characterise the dynamics rather than to evaluate the benchmarks, but it does
mean the rates should not be read as how often each motif would appear in
arbitrary deployment, and establishing that would require running the unscreened
task set. The study is also exploratory and reports many tests across twelve
configuration cells and four motifs, so we have leaned throughout on effect sizes
and on consistency across cells rather than on isolated significance, and the
central claims rest on effects that are large and repeated rather than on any
single borderline test.
